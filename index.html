💡 Shopping tips: pay the full price and claim tax back at Narita customs on 8 Jan (keep tax-free items unopened, in hand luggage, and show your passport or Visit Japan Web QR when you buy). Knives and liquids over 100 ml (sake, whisky) go in checked luggage. Check your home country's duty-free limits for alcohol. Leave space in the suitcase!

🥢 How eating out works

  • Ticket machines at ramen and gyudon shops: pay at the machine by the door, hand the ticket to the staff. Photos help; Google Translate camera reads the buttons.
  • Queues are normal and move fast. A line of locals is a good sign.
  • Reservations for nicer places (Kobe beef, Christmas Eve, NYE, farewell dinner): use the restaurant's site, TableCheck or Tabelog, or ask your hotel reception.
  • Izakaya (Japanese pubs) often add a small cover charge with an otoshi starter. That's normal, not a scam.
  • Water and tea are free. You'll get a hot or cold towel (oshibori) to wipe your hands.
  • Slurping noodles is fine. Don't stick chopsticks upright in rice or pass food chopstick to chopstick.
  • Paying: take the bill to the register at the front. No tipping.
  • 1–3 Jan: many small restaurants close. Chains, hotel restaurants, department-store food halls (from 2 Jan) and konbini stay open.
  • If you avoid pork or alcohol: pork is in most ramen broths (tonkotsu), gyoza and buns, and mirin/sake are common in sauces. Look for chicken or seafood ramen, sushi, tempura, soba/udon and halal-certified places (search "halal" in Google Maps).

🗣 Useful phrases

  • Itadakimasu: said before eating.
  • Gochisōsama deshita: thank you for the meal (when leaving).
  • Osusume wa?: what do you recommend?
  • Kore o kudasai: this one, please (point at the menu).
  • Futari desu: two people.
  • Okaikei onegaishimasu: the bill, please.
  • Kanpai!: cheers!
  • Oishii!: delicious!

🏪 Konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart)

  • Great, cheap breakfast and late snacks: onigiri (rice balls), egg sando, fried chicken (Famichiki, L-chiki), hot oden in winter, melon pan, and puddings.
  • Staff will heat bento for you. Open 24/7, including New Year's Day.

Cash plan
  • Carry about ¥10,000–20,000 each in cash. Cards work in most hotels, shops, stations and restaurants.
  • Cash needed for: shrines and temples, market stalls, small restaurants, some taxis, coin lockers.
  • Withdraw at a 7-Eleven ATM (open around the clock, English menu, there are some at Narita), or at Japan Post or Aeon ATMs.
  • When the ATM or card machine asks, always choose to pay in yen, not euro. The euro option uses a worse rate.
  • Before the trip: get a Wise or Revolut card, tell your bank you're travelling, and bring two cards (one Visa, one Mastercard) kept in different places.
  • Withdraw before 31 Dec so you're covered over New Year. Take out bigger amounts less often to save on fees.
  • There's no tipping in Japan. Bring a small coin purse for ¥100 and ¥500 coins.

🚆 Getting around

! RESERVE marks trains that need a reserved seat: only the Narita Express (21 Dec & 30 Dec) and the Rapi:t if you take it (30 Dec). Your Shinkansen (5 Jan) is already booked. Every other train, subway and bus: just tap Suica, no reservation.

  • IC card: add Suica or PASMO to your phone wallet, or buy a Welcome Suica at the airport. It works on trains, buses and in convenience stores in all three cities.
  • Shinjuku → Narita (30 Dec): Narita Express from Shinjuku takes about 80–90 min. Take a train around 10:00 for the 13:40 flight.
  • KIX → Higobashi: Nankai Rapi:t or the airport express to Namba, then the Yotsubashi subway line to Higobashi (about 1 h 15 min). The airport limousine bus is the other option.
  • Osaka → Kyoto (3 Jan): JR Special Rapid from Osaka Station takes about 30 min. 2–4 Jan is the return-travel peak, so go mid-morning.
  • Kyoto → KIX (7 Jan): the JR Haruka express leaves from Kyoto Station, next to your hotel (about 75–80 min). Take one around 16:30 for the 20:05 flight.
  • Domestic flights: low-cost carriers have strict baggage limits. Check what's included on both the NRT→KIX and KIX→NRT tickets.

🙇 Etiquette

  • Trains are quiet: no phone calls, low voices.
  • Escalators: stand left in Tokyo, right in Osaka; follow the crowd in Kyoto.
  • Few public bins: carry your rubbish back with you.
  • Shoes off when you see a raised step or slippers at the entrance.
  • Don't eat while walking in busy streets; eat at the stall.
  • At shrines: bow, coin, bow twice, clap twice, pray, bow once.
  • No tipping, ever. Onsen often refuse tattoos.

❄ Winter tips

  • Expect about 2–12 °C. Days are dry and often sunny, which is the best season for clear views of Mt Fuji. Wear layers. Heattech-style base layers from Uniqlo are cheap and very good.
  • Sunset is around 16:30. Plan outdoor sights for the morning and keep illuminations and city views for evening.
  • Carry some cash for shrine stalls, small restaurants and offerings at hatsumode (a 5-yen coin is traditionally lucky).
  • Shoes come off in temples and some restaurants, so bring socks without holes.

🆘 Emergencies

  • 110 police · 119 ambulance & fire.
  • Japan Visitor Hotline (JNTO, 24 h, English): 050-3816-2787.
